# Trailer typography, clichés and video legibility (web research)

Scope: Latin-script type for game/film trailers, genre clichés, OFL alternatives, and on-screen legibility rules. Japanese typography and measured teaser data are covered by other notes. Research date: 2026-11-05.

## 1. Trailer and poster typography: history and current use

### Takeaway

- Trajan (Carol Twombly, Adobe, 1989) is the canonical "epic movie" face and is now widely treated as the "Arial of movie posters". Big studios moved away from it; A24 built identity partly by avoiding it. For shotpilot: Trajan-like caps (Cinzel) read as a stock-prestige signal, not a distinctive one. [S]
- Modern film title treatment is mostly geometric or grotesque sans (Gotham Bold is cited for Oppenheimer; Futura for Kubrick/2001/Life of Pi) or custom lettering. [S snippet]
- Game logos are custom lettering; stock fonts appear in supers, callouts and CTAs. [S snippet]
- Sci-fi supers and UI: Microgramma/Eurostile (Novarese/Butti, 1950s-62) is the historic shorthand; Ray Larabie calls the related Handel Gothic "really overused" in sci-fi games, though "not technically wrong". [S snippet]

### Cited findings

- Trajan origin and rise: designed 1989 by Carol Twombly from Trajan's Column; bundled with early Photoshop; on posters for At Play in the Fields of the Lord, The Bodyguard and Scent of a Woman by 1992. Yves Peters (FontShop) says it became the default for "epic" films and that overuse made it "the Arial of movie posters"; he recommends custom hand-drawn lettering as the way out. [S] https://avclub.com/heres-how-a-single-font-came-to-dominate-movie-posters-1827234582
- Trajan was already "over-used to death on countless movie trailer and posters" in a December 2007 post. [S] https://www.slashfilm.com/trajan-the-movie-font
- "When in doubt, use Trajan"; Kirby Ferguson's 2011 video and Drew Lytle's 2014 supercut made the joke mainstream; A24 (2012) built its identity partly on not using Trajan. [S snippet] https://filmfont.pages.dev/articles/trajan-most-overused-movie-poster-font ; https://kottke.org/18/07/how-trajan-became-the-go-to-typeface-for-movie-posters ; https://atissuejournal.com/page/7
- Current frequency check: a self-described pilot dataset (20 posters/title cards, 2014-2024) found Trajan-family type in 2 of 20 (both modified, for Dune) and all 4 A24 entries on custom or geometric sans. Not trailer-specific and tiny. [S snippet] https://filmfont.pages.dev/research/movie-title-font-frequency-2026/
- Gotham: the claim that Gotham is "the current font for title cards in film trailers in the US" (MPA) appears only on student portfolios and Wikipedia mirrors; the Oppenheimer title is commonly identified as Gotham Bold. Unverified against MPA. [S snippet] https://www.wikipedia.com/wiki/Gotham_(typeface) ; https://dev.to/abuhasnat/what-fonts-were-used-in-oppenheimer-dune-furiosa-and-other-recent-film-posters-231g
- Bebas Neue is a free (OFL) condensed all-caps display face by Ryoichi Tsunekawa, often used for film/video titles, but I found no evidence that it is "the trailer font". [S snippet] https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Bebas+Neue/about
- Sci-fi: 2001 uses Futura (title cards, screens, credits) plus Eurostile Bold Extended; Alien's title is disputed (Futura variant vs Helvetica Black); Microgramma and variants run through the Alien films; Blade Runner (1982) does not use Eurostile (serifs, neo-noir). Source is Dave Addey's Typeset in the Future. [S snippet] https://typesetinthefuture.com/2001-a-space-odyssey/ ; https://typesetinthefuture.com/category/font-family/futura/ ; https://blog.frame.io/2017/12/11/8-sci-fi-fonts/
- Games in Fonts In Use (snippets only): Uppercut Angle for the TMNT: Splintered Fate trailer call-to-action (https://fontsinuse.com/uses/62721/teenage-mutant-ninja-turtles-splintered-fate); the Geometry Dash trailer pairs Oxygene with Pusab (https://fontsinuse.com/uses/69646/geometry-dash-video-game); Cyberpunk 2077 uses a custom face after long experimentation, per a Game Informer summary (https://www.fontsinuse.com/uses/60926/cyberpunk-2077-video-game). Entries exist for Death Stranding, The Last of Us, ARC Raiders, Cronos: The New Dawn and Titanfall 2 on news.abstractfonts.com mirrors, not read. [S snippet]
- A game-UI team chose Futura because the font "had to be expressive, but not too much" so as not to slow reading. [S snippet] https://www.pcgamer.com/how-game-developers-pick-their-fonts
- Not found: review-quote/laurel conventions and date/platform end-slate typography sources. [I] From memory: end slates use a neutral grotesque or the logo's companion sans in caps with generous tracking, small relative to the logo. Verify against the measured-teaser notes.

## 2. Clichés and overused fonts by genre

### Takeaway

- Every genre has a stock face that signals "genre" but not "this game": fantasy Trajan/Cinzel gold caps; sci-fi Orbitron/Eurostile/Handel Gothic/Audiowide; horror drip/blood faces and distressed typewriter; retro Press Start 2P/VT323; action Bebas/Impact; cozy Fredoka/Baloo-type bubbles. Direct evidence that designers call these clichés is strong for Trajan, moderate for Handel Gothic/Orbitron and blood-drip, and absent for the rest (those entries are [I]).
- Horror practice moves toward restraint: a thin serif in a dark field beats dripping lettering, and real horror title work breaks rules (uneven stroke, ink bleed) rather than using a "horror font". Low-authority sources. [S snippet]
- open-design bans its list because LLMs converge on the same fonts, producing monoculture across outputs. This matters here: shotpilot's looks currently name Inter, Cinzel and Cormorant Garamond (all on the ban list) and Lora/Oswald/Rajdhani/Fredoka (popular, not banned).

### Cited findings

- open-design `design-templates/hyperframes/references/typography.md` (read via gh) [S]:
  - Banned "training-data defaults": Inter, Roboto, Open Sans, Noto Sans, Arimo, Lato, Source Sans, PT Sans, Nunito, Poppins, Outfit, Sora, Playfair Display, Cormorant Garamond, Bodoni Moda, EB Garamond, Cinzel, Prata, Syne ("Syne in particular is the most overused 'distinctive' display font. It is an instant AI design tell.").
  - Guardrails: do not pair two sans-serifs (cross serif+sans or sans+mono); one expressive font per scene; weight contrast 300 vs 900 not 400 vs 700; video sizes: body 20px min, headlines 60px+, data labels 16px; 3 s on screen must be readable in 2 s; tracking -0.03 to -0.05em on display sizes because "video encoding compresses letter detail".
  - Dark backgrounds: light-on-dark looks heavier (use 350 not 400 for body) and tighter (line-height +0.05-0.1; display +0.01em tracking).
  - Selection method: name the register first, think of a physical object, reject the first instinct, cross-check the category assumption. Its font-query script filters Google Fonts metadata by dateAdded and popularity and excludes the ban list.
  - Apparent contradiction (-0.03 to -0.05em vs +0.01em on dark). Resolve as: tight for mixed-case display, neutral to open for caps and small text. [I]
- Ray Larabie: Handel Gothic is "really overused" in sci-fi games though not technically wrong. [S snippet] https://themarysue.com/?p=24806 (page returned 403)
- Orbitron called the unofficial standard for futuristic game logos; a Bleeding-Cowboys-style gothic called overused in horror and Halloween. Low-authority listicles. [S snippet] https://madegooddesigns.com/best-fonts-for-game-titles/
- Game Developer article: avoid engine defaults (Arial, Times, Courier), Comic Sans, Papyrus; display faces work in small doses (a few words); text needs contrast; centered text rarely ideal beyond a few words. [S] https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/down-to-the-letter-the-importance-of-typography-in-video-games
- Blood-drip: "Ask whether the effect supports the story or just looks spooky." Vendor/SEO pages, weak. [S snippet] https://designbeep.com/2025/07/21/20-best-horror-movie-fonts/ . Licensing: many free blood fonts are personal-use only; the Google Fonts versions (Creepster, Nosifer, Eater, Butcherman) are OFL, so licence is not the issue there.
- Not found: any source naming Creepster, Press Start 2P, VT323, Bebas/Impact or Fredoka as clichés. These are [I]. Indirect evidence of ubiquity is Google Fonts popularity rank from the metadata JSON fetched 2026-11-05 [S]: Inter 5, Roboto Condensed 14, Oswald 19, Nunito 25, Bebas Neue 43, Playfair Display 24, Cinzel 120, Orbitron 127, Fredoka 123, Rajdhani 122, Press Start 2P 236, Creepster 319, VT323 302.

## 3. Alternatives (Google Fonts, OFL)

Method: metadata from https://fonts.google.com/metadata/fonts (fetched 2026-11-05) for category, popularity rank (lower = more used), dateAdded, weights. All listed families exist in that metadata (Google Fonts hosts open-licence fonts, OFL for nearly all). URL pattern: https://fonts.google.com/specimen/<Family+Name>. The J-pair notes are [I] (stroke contrast and x-height matched by reasoning, not tested with renders).

Pairing rules of thumb [I]: a high-contrast Latin serif (Fraunces, Gloock, Instrument Serif) pairs with mincho (Shippori Mincho, Noto Serif JP, Zen Antique); a monoline grotesque pairs with a gothic (Zen Kaku Gothic New, Noto Sans JP); condensed Latin is narrower than full-width kana/kanji, so set it 10-15% larger than a JP line for equal visual weight; low-x-height Latin (Cormorant, Cinzel) looks tiny beside Japanese, one more reason not to use it for sub-lines.

## 4. Legibility of text in video

### Takeaway

- Safe areas: EBU R95 graphics safe area is a 5% margin on each side (90% x 90% of frame), action safe 3.5%. At 1920x1080 that is 96 px at the sides and 54 px top/bottom for graphics (arithmetic, [I]). [S]
- Size: broadcast/online subtitle authoring uses line height of 7-8% of picture height (about 76-86 px at 1080p; BBC). Players then scale down to 0.6-0.8x on larger screens, never up. A 4 in phone is x1, a 5.5 in phone x0.67. [S]
- Open-design's video floor (body 20 px, headline 60 px+, data labels 16 px) is for composited motion graphics and is far below BBC caption size; for burned-in captions that must read on a phone, plan on about 5-6% of frame height (54-65 px) minimum. [I] from BBC numbers plus vendor blogs suggesting 40-60 px at 1080p and 60 px for phones [S snippet].
- Hairlines and thin serifs: advice to avoid thin horizontal strokes comes from interlaced-era broadcast flicker (Apple Motion manual). H.264/YouTube behaviour is NOT documented in anything I found. The stroke-vs-compression claim is [I]: quantisation smears fine detail and 4:2:0 chroma softens colored edges, so keep strokes at least about 2 px at delivered 1080p and use white/near-white on dark.
- Chroma subsampling: 4:2:0 shares one color sample per 2x2 pixels, so saturated colored text (red) gets blurry fringes; white and black text keep full-resolution luma. A luma outline helps. [S snippet]
- Steam: the small capsule is the legibility floor; design it to hold at 120 px wide; 0-3 words and only the logo. Text in the autoplay trailer must survive similar downscaling. [S]

### Cited findings

- EBU R95 v1.1 (June 2017): "The action safe area is 3.5% and the graphics safe area is 5%, at the top, bottom and lateral parts of the image"; defined in lines and pixels for 576, 720, 1080, 2160 and 4320 rasters (read with pdftotext). [S] https://tech.ebu.ch/docs/r/r095.pdf . The 1080p pixel figures sit in figure images that I could not extract. SMPTE title-safe "90%" appears only in secondary sources. [S snippet] https://convertaudiototext.com/blog/subtitle-styling-best-practices
- BBC subtitle guidelines, read via a mirror (bbc.github.io URL returned 404; verify against the BBC page before quoting): authoring line height 7-8% of active video height for 16:9, 4:3, 1:1 (7.5% acceptable) and 3.9-4.5% for 9:16; do not scale up on playback; preferred 0.6-0.8x; multipliers 4 in phone x1, 5.5 in phone x0.67, 7 in tablet x0.8, 32-42 in TV x0.67, laptop x0.6; max 2 lines (3 for vertical); broadcast 37 characters per line; online line length 68% of 16:9 width; online safe area 16:9 central 90% vertical, central 75% horizontal; authoring font wide (Reith Sans, Verdana, Tiresias); default white on black; per-line background extends 0.5 em each side with no gaps. [S] https://bbcbreakingnews.pages.dev/accessibility/forproducts/guides/subtitles
- Netflix timed text: centred, top or bottom, max 2 lines, event minimum 5/6 s (20 frames at 24 fps), maximum 7 s; size is a TTML percentage, no px. 42 characters per line for most Latin languages (template guide; not auto-enforced per FAQ). Japanese guide: 13 full-width characters per line horizontal, 11 vertical. [S/S snippet] https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/215758617 ; https://backlothelp.netflix.com/hc/en-us/articles/215767517
- Thin type and flicker: Apple Motion manual says avoid single-pixel horizontal lines and fonts that are too thin on interlaced video; David Earls: avoid fonts with thin horizontal lines. The serif prohibition is CRT-era folklore and one poster argues it need not apply. [S snippet] https://help.apple.com/motion/mac/5.0/help/English/en/motion/usermanual/chapter_B_section_6.html ; https://luc.devroye.org/fonts-43098.html ; https://forum.videohelp.com/showthread.php?p=2459893
- Chroma: 4:2:0 shares color per 2x2 block; white/black text unaffected; outlined text recommended by a forum poster; extra source resolution partly helps. [S snippet] https://forum.videohelp.com/threads/412049-Text-problems-on-video ; https://www.forasoft.com/learn/video-encoding/glossary/terms/chroma-subsampling
- Steam capsules (third-party guide; check Valve partner docs): Header 920x430, Small 462x174, Main 1232x706, Vertical 748x896, Library 600x900; legacy 460x215 and 231x87 still render in places; Steam derives 184x69 and 120x45 from the small capsule; best indie capsules use 0-3 words; review quotes unreadable at thumbnail scale. [S] https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-capsule-design-guide . Another guide: small-capsule logo should nearly fill the frame. [S snippet] https://fantasygroundsunity.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/FGCP/pages/2156462081/Guidelines+-+Product+Store+Images
- Phone overlays: vendor blogs suggest 40-60 px captions at 1080p and a 60 px minimum on a 1080x1920 frame; no platform source. [S snippet] https://blitzcutai.com/blog/best-caption-size-youtube-shorts-2026 . YouTube lets viewers set caption size on-device. [S snippet] https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7364677
- Not found: any YouTube or Steam statement on re-encode behaviour for thin strokes; any written outline/shadow convention beyond "an outline helps"; all-caps tracking numbers beyond open-design's display tracking. Values marked [I] should be checked by encoding a test card at CRF 23-28 and viewing it on a phone.

### (b) Legibility rules for video

| Rule | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Graphics safe area | 5% inset per side (90% x 90%); 96 px / 54 px at 1920x1080 | EBU R95 [S]; px by arithmetic [I] |
| Action safe area | 3.5% inset | EBU R95 [S] |
| Online subtitle safe area, 16:9 | central 90% vertical, central 75% horizontal | BBC (mirror) [S] |
| Caption line height, 16:9 | 7-8% of picture height (about 76-86 px at 1080p authored); players scale 0.6-0.8x | BBC (mirror) [S] |
| Phone worst case | x1 on a 4 in phone, x0.67 on 5.5 in, so authored size is what a phone sees | BBC (mirror) [S] |

| Caption duration | min 5/6 s (20 frames at 24 fps), max 7 s | Netflix [S] |
| Reading time | on screen 3 s must read in 2 s | open-design [S] |

| Stroke weight | at least about 2 px strokes at delivered 1080p; avoid hairlines and thin horizontals | Apple/Earls [S snippet]; px [I] |
